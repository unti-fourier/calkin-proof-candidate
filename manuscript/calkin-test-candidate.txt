THE FIXED CALKIN TENSOR TEST AND A POSITIVE KERNEL CONSTRUCTION
An unreviewed proof candidate

ABSTRACT

Let Q=B(ell2)/K(ell2) be the Calkin algebra on a fixed separable
infinite-dimensional Hilbert space. This manuscript proposes a proof
that a complex C* algebra B is nuclear precisely when the canonical map
B tensor_max Q -> B tensor_min Q is injective. No unitality, separability,
sigma-unitality or initial exactness of B is assumed. The argument first
extracts a finite unitary family and one normal state in a literal corner
of B**. A cyclic-lamp construction then gives a single completely positive
moment map defined on the whole Calkin algebra. A Hilbert--Schmidt frame
estimate supplies its normal source extension. Finally finite normal
tests select coefficients in B itself, and exactness supplies one ideal
cut preserving a fixed state. The proposed positive kernel element has
state value greater than 27/256 and norm at most 1/8.

Status. The main theorem, Propositions G, M and D, and Lemma HS below
are proposed results, with proofs supplied here. They are not presented as established
theorems. The established results adopted as inputs are listed separately
in Section 1. Mathematical correctness of the new arguments remains to be
independently assessed. All mathematical dependencies used in the proof
are either stated standard inputs or proved in this manuscript.

0. STATEMENT, CONVENTIONS AND NOTATION

Fix H_*=ell2(N), Q_*=B(H_*)/K(H_*). For any complex C* algebra B let
 chi_B:B tensor_max Q_* -> B tensor_min Q_*
be the canonical surjective *-homomorphism extending the identity on
B odot Q_*. The symbol odot denotes the algebraic tensor product.

Main theorem (proposed). For every such B,
          chi_B is injective if and only if B is nuclear.        (T)

This is the fixed-Calkin tensor test considered here. Injectivity means
equality of the two completed C* norms, since an injective *-homomorphism
is isometric. It does not require equality of norms in every individual
representation. The implication from nuclearity follows from the
definition of nuclearity. The case B=0 is immediate. We prove the other
implication for arbitrary nonzero B, using Section 1 to obtain exactness.

All Hilbert-space inner products are linear in the first variable.
The conjugate C* algebra bar(N) has conjugated scalar multiplication;
it is not being identified silently with the opposite algebra. In a
normal standard form of N on H_N, L_N is the faithful left action,
J_N the conjugation, and
 r_N(x)=J_N L_N(x*)J_N,  r_N(x)r_N(y)=r_N(yx).
For a normal state phi, xi_phi is its unit vector in the natural cone.
A UCP map is unital and completely positive; normality of a positive map
between von Neumann algebras means preservation of bounded increasing
positive nets, equivalently ultraweak continuity. A trace called n.s.f.
is normal, semifinite and faithful. N_* denotes the normal predual,
whereas N* denotes the full Banach dual. Corners have their relative
units. Indexed families retain repetitions.
All strong/ultraweak limits may be nets unless a sequence is specified.
No target Hilbert space or predual is assumed separable.

The proposed proof has three components. G supplies one corner, one tuple and
one normal state from nonnuclearity. M supplies one state-independent
moment map, a faithful source copy and exact block partitions from that
tuple. D uses these same objects to construct the nonzero positive
kernel element over original B. These components are proved in Sections
2,3,4, respectively; none is invoked merely as an additional hypothesis
in the final assembly. Section 5 records their order of use.

1. STANDARD INPUTS AND EXACTNESS FROM THE HYPOTHESIS

Recall that exactness of B means that tensoring every C* short exact
sequence with B in the minimal norm preserves exactness. Nuclearity
means equality of minimal and maximal tensor norms with every C* algebra.
We adopt the following established results at their stated scopes.
 (E) For every C* algebra B, exactness is equivalent to exactness of
     0->B min K(H_*)->B min B(H_*)->B min Q_*->0.
     Kirchberg [1], Theorem 1.1(ii),(iii).
 (B) B nuclear iff its canonical bidual B** is injective, also for
     nonunital and nonseparable B. Choi--Effros [2]. An arbitrary weak
     closure of a faithful C* representation is not substituted for B**.
 (H) A von Neumann algebra F0 is finite and injective iff for every
     finite unitary family w_1,...,w_k and nonzero central projection p,
     ||sum_j p w_j tensor overline(p w_j)||min=k.
     Haagerup [3], Lemma 2.2. We use this only when F0 is already finite.
 (S) Standard forms exist on arbitrary von Neumann algebras. Every
     normal positive functional has a unique natural-cone vector, with
     ||xi_phi-xi_psi||^2<=||phi-psi||. The standard implementation
     L(u)r(u*) of an inner automorphism preserves that cone.
     Haagerup [4], Theorem 1.6 and Lemma 2.10.
 (C) For a sigma-finite M and faithful normal state chi, let
     C0=M crossed_(sigma^chi) R, beta its dual action, C2=C0 crossed_beta R.
     C0 has a normal semifinite faithful trace, and C2 is normally,
     faithfully and onto *-isomorphic to M spatial B(L2(R)), with normal
     inverse. No factor or separable-H assumption is imposed. [5],[6].

We also use ordinary C*/von Neumann theory: Arveson extension into B(H),
CP Schwarz, GNS and continuous functional calculus, normal supports,
the semifinite/typeIII central split, semifinite trace theory and proper
infinite halving; faithful min inclusions and maximal commuting
representations; source standard left/right commutants and Kaplansky
density; Hahn--Banach, Goldstine and weak* compactness. In particular
no extension into an arbitrary noninjective commutant is assumed.
Arveson extension here concerns a UCP map from a unital operator system
into B(H), with arbitrary H. Kaplansky density concerns the bounded
unit ball and positive matrix cones of a C* algebra in its von Neumann
closure. Goldstine concerns the weak* density of Ball(B) in Ball(B**)
for the specified predual B*. These applications are spelled out below.

Exactness gateway. Maximal tensor products preserve short exact rows
for an IDEAL J in A. For isometry B max J->B max A, extend a commuting
representation of J to A on its essential J-space by an approximate
identity; it still commutes with B. The orthogonal complement contributes
zero to B odot J. A quotient representation annihilating B max J factors
through A/J on the joint essential space. These two universal properties
give the maximal exact row; no arbitrary-subalgebra max inclusion is used.

Every canonical max-to-min map is onto: its range is closed and contains
the dense algebraic tensor product. Assume chi_B injective. Lift an
element t of the kernel of the minimal
B(H_*) quotient row through B max B(H_*)->B min B(H_*). The maximal
quotient image of the lift has zero minimal image, hence is zero by
chi_B's injectivity. Maximal ideal exactness puts the lift in B max K;
its minimal image is in B min K. The reverse inclusion is immediate,
so the minimal test row is exact. Input(E) gives exactness of original B,
with no separability or unitization
step. This argument does not first assert the hypothesis for a subalgebra
or an amplification of B.

2. GENERAL INPUT G IN ONE LITERAL CORNER

Proposition G (proposed).
Let V be a noninjective von Neumann algebra, with no separability or
sigma-finiteness assumption. Given 0<q0<1 and epsilon>0, there are:
 a nonzero e in Proj(V), the literal algebra N=eVe;
 a finite F with faithful normal tracial state tau;
 a normal faithful relative-unital *-embedding i:F->N;
 a relative UCP return E:N->F, Ei=id_F, not assumed normal;
 a finite indexed tuple v_1,...,v_n in i(U(F));
 one normal state phi on N, not necessarily faithful,
such that
 q=||n^-1 sum_j v_j tensor bar(v_j)||_(N min bar(N))<=q0,
 max_j ||phi composed Ad(v_j*)-phi||_(N_*)<epsilon.          (G)
Here bar(N) is the conjugate C* algebra; q is an operator norm, not its
square. With the standard form L_N,J_N and r_N(x)=J_N L_N(x*)J_N,
 r=n^-1 sum_j Re<L_N(v_j)r_N(v_j*)xi_phi,xi_phi>
   >1-epsilon/2.                                         (G-CORR)
Thus q0=1/4 and epsilon=1/32 give q<1/3 and r>63/64 on the same data.

Proof. Adopt the standard inputs(B),(H),(S),(C) from Section 1.

2.1 Localization without a separable representation

Represent V faithfully normally as Q subset B(K), allowing arbitrary K.
Injectivity is equivalent to a UCP map B(K)->Q fixing Q. The forward
implication extends id_Q; the converse extends a map into B(K) and then
composes. This also proves independence of the faithful normal copy.
The point-ultraweak compact space UCP(B(K),Q) is nonempty: it contains
X->varpi(X)1. Linearity, unit and all matrix-positive constraints are
closed in the product of weak* compact coordinate balls. Noninjectivity
makes the closed constraints nu(T(x))=nu(x), for all selfadjoint
contractions x in Q and normal states nu, have empty intersection.
A finite subfamily already has empty intersection. Let s be the support
of the sum of those finitely many states. Their normalized sum is a
faithful normal state on sQs. If sQs were injective, a return R_s on
B(sK) would make T(X)=R_s(sXs)+varpi(X)(1-s) a UCP map satisfying all
selected tests, since nu(sxs)=nu(x). Contradiction. Pull back s to V.
This is a literal sigma-finite noninjective corner, without selecting
factor fibers or a common exceptional null set. Split its semifinite
and typeIII central parts. One is noninjective: returns for both parts
would give a return for their direct sum. Call that part M=p_M V p_M.

Another compactness fact will be used twice. If p_a increases strongly
to1 in Q and every p_a Q p_a is injective, the maps
 T_a(X)=R_a(p_a X p_a)+varpi(X)(1-p_a)
have a cofinal point-ultraweak cluster map fixing Q, since on x in Q
their values are p_a x p_a+varpi(x)(1-p_a)->x. Thus Q is injective.
For an n.s.f. trace, finite-trace projections are directed under joins
(Tr(p join q)<=Tr(p)+Tr(q)) and have supremum1 by semifiniteness.
Therefore a noninjective semifinite algebra has a noninjective
finite-trace corner. No sequence is substituted for this general net.

If M is semifinite, choose f with 0<Tr(f)<infinity and fMf noninjective.
Take F0=N0=fVf, e0=f, i0=R0=id and tau0=Tr|F0/Tr(f). Continue with Section 2.4.

2.2 TypeIII: exact copies, bounded returns and a literal target

Fix a faithful normal chi of M for its modular action only. Put
 j0:M->C0, j1:C0->C2, the normal faithful canonical unital embeddings;
 beta=the first dual action, gamma=the second dual action.
Their fixed algebras are j0(M) and j1(C0), respectively. For clarity,
this fixed-copy identification needs no separable Hilbert space: in the
regular gauge of a spatially implemented R-action alpha on Q, the
product is {Q tensor1, V_t tensor lambda_t}'', lambda_t f(s)=f(s-t).
The dual is Ad(1 tensor m_h), m_h f(s)=exp(ihs)f(s). Every product
element commutes with scalar translations. A dual-fixed element also
commutes with scalar modulations, hence equals x tensor1. For y in Q',
the multiplier Y_y(s)=V_s y V_s* commutes with both generating families.
Thus [x,V_s y V_s*] has zero coefficients almost everywhere for each
fixed vector pair. Those coefficients are continuous; evaluate at0 to
obtain [x,y]=0. Hence x in Q. Standard form gives the required strongly
continuous spatial implementation for each action.

For eta=beta or gamma, define the normal UCP averages
 A_L^eta(X)=(2L)^-1 integral_[-L,L] eta_t(X)dt.
The ultraweak integral is normal: predual orbits are norm-continuous,
as follows from strong unitary implementation and trace-class
approximation in a normal representation. Moreover
 ||eta_h(A_L^eta(X))-A_L^eta(X)||<=|h| ||X||/L.
A cofinal point-ultraweak cluster limit P_eta is UCP into the fixed
copy and fixes that entire copy. Its normality is not inferred. Set
 E0=j0^-1 P_beta:C0->M, E0 j0=id_M;
 E1=j1^-1 P_gamma:C2->C0, E1 j1=id_C0.                (RET)
For every a,b in C0 and Y in C2, the multiplicative domain gives
 E1(j1(a)Yj1(b))=a E1(Y)b.                           (MOD)
E0 is used here only to reflect noninjectivity: if C0 were injective,
extension into C0 followed by E0 would make M injective. Thus C0 is
noninjective. This is a retract argument, not inheritance by arbitrary
subalgebras.

Let D_dual:C2->M spatial B(L2(R)) be the normal onto duality isomorphism.
In sigma-finite typeIII M, halve its unit into two projections equivalent
to it, retain the half of smaller chi-mass, and repeat. Remainder masses
tend to0; faithfulness/normality of chi makes their meet zero. The removed
pieces are orthogonal and equivalent to1_M, with sum1_M. Choose isometries
s_l with these ranges. U(eta tensor delta_l)=s_l eta is a unitary
K_M tensor ell2 -> K_M. For A_M(X)=UXU*,
 A_M(x tensor e_ij)=s_i x s_j* in M.
Finite matrix compressions and strong closure give its range in M.
Conversely U* x U has entries s_i* x s_j in M, and the same compression
argument puts it in M spatial B(ell2). Thus A_M is normal and onto,
with normal inverse. A fixed unitary
L2(R)->ell2 and D_dual now give a normal onto *-isomorphism Xi:C2->M.
It need not fix j1j0(M); it does map 1_C2 to1_M=p_M.

3. THE CYCLIC-LAMP MOMENT MAP M

Proposition M (proposed, state-independent moment bridge). Let N be any von Neumann
algebra in standard form and let v_1,...,v_n be relative unitaries with
 q=||n^-1 sum_j v_j tensor bar(v_j)||_(N min bar(N))<1/3, n>=1.
Put G=F(s,t), Gamma=(Z/(n^2))^(G) semidirect G, with finite-support
lamps, C=C*_r(Gamma), D=L(Gamma). Identify each cyclic lamp's n^2
spectral atoms with pairs in {0,...,n-1}^2; partition labels j run from1
to n. Its row and column partitions
at h are p_(h,s,j),p_(h,t,j); let d_(u,j)=w_u p_(1,u,j), u=s,t.
Fix any one countable sequence of group homomorphisms rho_l:Gamma->U(d_l)
whose normalized traces tend to the regular Gamma trace, and one
free ultrafilter omega. Set
 P=product_l M_(d_l), I={b:lim_omega tr_l(b_l*b_l)=0}, U=P/I,
 H_c=direct_sum_l C^(d_l), Q_c=B(H_c)/K(H_c), q_tau:P->U the quotient.
Here j:P->Q_c is block-diagonal inclusion followed by the Calkin quotient.
Let S_u and P_(u,j) be the model shifts and lamp partitions and put
b_(u,j)=S_u P_(u,j). The shifts are unitaries and the P_(u,j) form
exact orthogonal partitions in P. There is a faithful unital embedding
 sigma_C:C->U, sigma_C(d_(u,j))=q_tau(b_(u,j)),
and there is one UCP map
 T_v:Q_c->L_N(N)'
such that, simultaneously,
 T_v(j(b_(u,j)))=r_N(v_j*)/n,   T_v(j(i))=0 for every i in I.       (M)
No target state, original B, or later ideal cut is involved in choosing
this map. It is not asserted multiplicative, normally lifted to B(H_c),
or onto the target commutant. Its specified values, not just range
containment, are essential. The proof below supplies such a sequence
and proves the conclusion for
any one fixed sequence satisfying the stated trace condition.

Proof.

3.1 The source group and its cocycle.

The lamp group consists of finite-support functions a:G->Z/(n^2).
With (g.b)(h)=b(g^-1 h), its semidirect product law and inverse are
 (a,g)(b,h)=(a+g.b,gh), (a,g)^-1=(-g^-1.a,g^-1).
The Fourier dual of the lamp group is
Omega=({0,...,n-1}^2)^G with uniform product measure. Put R=L-infinity(Omega). At site h, p_(h,s,j) and p_(h,t,j)
are the indicators of row j-1 and column j-1. The cyclic character has
value exp(2 pi i (n a+b)/n^2) on the pair(a,b). Thus the lamp is the
original cyclic group of order n^2, not the group C_n times C_n.
Write sigma_g p_(h,u,j)=p_(gh,u,j), w_g a w_g*=sigma_g(a). Then
 D=R crossed_regular G=L(Gamma), A=C[Gamma] consists of finite sums
 x=sum_g a_g w_g with finite cylinder a_g, and C=C*_r(Gamma).
Here (a w_g)(b w_h)=a sigma_g(b)w_gh and
(a w_g)*=sigma_(g^-1)(a*)w_(g^-1). Its faithful regular trace satisfies
tau_D(a w_g)=0 for g!=1 and tau_D(a)=integral a dmu; thus
||x||2^2=sum_g||a_g||2^2. Finite cyclic characters form the exact
regular group orthonormal basis, not merely an approximate family.

For reduced g=u_1^eps1 ... u_l^epsl, let g_i be its prefixes, g_0=1.
Edge i has key(g_i,u_i) if eps_i=+1 and key(g_(i-1),u_i) if eps_i=-1.
Key(h,u) denotes the unoriented edge h u^-1 ->h in either direction.
Put
 Q_(g,J)=product_i p_(key_i,j_i),
 V_(g,J)=v_j1^eps1 ... v_jl^epsl, B_g=sum_J Q_(g,J) tensor V_(g,J).
These are finite cylinder functions with unitary values in N. Reversing
an edge uses the same partition, not a fresh label. On an adjacent
backtrack the two projections force equal labels, and adjacent vv* or
v*v cancels without moving other factors. Translation sends(h,u) to
(gh,u). Repeated adjacent cancellation therefore proves for all words
 B_gh=B_g sigma_g(B_h),
 B_(g^-1 h)=sigma_(g^-1)(B_g* B_h).                    (COC)
This includes concatenations with an arbitrarily long cancellation.

On L2(R) let L_R(a) be left multiplication and r_R(a) right
multiplication; they coincide because R is abelian. The unitary S_g is
specified by S_g hat(a)=hat(sigma_g(a)). On H_raw=L2(R) tensor H_N,
put CalR=r_R tensor r_N, a linear anti-representation on the finite
R-valued tensor sums used here, and define
 Pi_g=CalR(B_g*)(S_g tensor1), J0 eta=1 tensor eta.
Anti-multiplication and COC give Pi_g Pi_h=Pi_gh. Covariance with L_R
then gives a group *-representation pi_alg(a w_g)=L_R(a)Pi_g and hence
pi_f of the full C_f=C*(Gamma). Its UCP compression Phi=J0*pi_f(.)J0 has
range in L_N(N)' and on A is
 T0(a w_g)=integral a(omega) r_N(B_g(omega)*) dmu
          =sum_J tau_R(a Q_(g,J)) r_N(V_(g,J)*).        (FWD)
The integral is a finite sum; no measurability of nonseparable operator
spaces is assumed. For d_(u,j)=w_u p_(1,u,j)=p_(u,u,j)w_u it gives
                         T0(d_(u,j))=r_N(v_j*)/n.    (MOM)

3.2 Positivity at every matrix degree. If x=a w_g,y=b w_h, then
x*y=sigma_(g^-1)(a* b) w_(g^-1 h). Using COC and invariance of mu,
 T0(x*y)=integral overline(a)b r_N(B_h* B_g) dmu
         =integral A_x* A_y dmu, A_x=a r_N(B_g*).      (GRAM)
The matrix-entry order is B_h* B_g because r_N is anti-multiplicative.
For arbitrary rectangular Z=[z_li] with z_li=sum_g a_(li,g)w_g put
F_li=sum_g a_(li,g) r_N(B_g*). Then for every finite r,k,
 T0^(k)(Z*Z)=integral F*F,
 <T0^(k)(Z*Z)eta,eta>=sum_l integral||sum_i F_li eta_i||^2>=0. (CP-A)
Choose one finite set S of sites containing all coefficients' lamp
supports and all words' edge keys. The integral is precisely
           n^(-2|S|) sum_(omega_S) F(omega_S)*F(omega_S).
Each site receives one pair of labels across every row, column and word.
Shared edges share labels; refining S just sums unused probabilities to 1.
Thus all matrix sizes concern the same map, with every cross term kept.
This proves algebraic matrix-square positivity and is compatible with
the full compression Phi. It does not by itself prove reduced positivity
or normality; those are the next two steps.

3.3 A uniform Hilbert--Schmidt frame estimate

Lemma HS (proposed Hilbert--Schmidt frame estimate).

Let H be a nonzero complex Hilbert space, with no separability hypothesis,
and U_1,...,U_n unitaries, n>=1, with repetitions allowed. Inner products are
linear in the first variable; |eta><zeta| sends x to <x,zeta>eta. On
S2(H), the ambient Hilbert-Schmidt space, define
 P_+(X)=n^-1 sum_j U_j X U_j*,
 P_-(X)=n^-1 sum_j U_j* X U_j.
Assume q=||P_+||_(S2->S2)<1/3 and set C_q^2=(1+q)/(1-3q).
P_-=P_+* on S2, so its norm is also q. These are averages of unitary
conjugations ON S2; each has norm1 on B(H). No state or trace on a target
von Neumann algebra is assumed. The trace defining S2 is the ambient one.

Let G=F(s,t), Omega=({0,...,n-1}^2)^G with uniform product probability.
At a site h let (R_h,C_h) be its coordinate pair. This is the spectrum
of one cyclic lamp of order n^2, labeled by
 zeta_h=exp(2pi i(n R_h+C_h)/n^2).
The characters chi_alpha=product_h zeta_h^(alpha(h)), for finite-support
alpha:G->Z/(n^2), are its original cyclic Fourier basis. Relabeling the
uniform atom by a pair does not change the underlying cyclic lamp group.

For a reduced g=u_1^eps1 ... u_l^epsl, let g_i be its successive prefixes,
g_0=e, u_i in{s,t}, eps_i in{+1,-1}. Its i-th slot is (h_i,u_i), where
 h_i=g_i if eps_i=+1, h_i=g_(i-1) if eps_i=-1.
The key(h,u) denotes the unoriented edge h u^-1--h, with that fixed
positive orientation. Use j_i=1+R_(h_i) for u_i=s and j_i=1+C_(h_i)
for u_i=t, and define
 W_g=U_(j_1)^eps1 ... U_(j_l)^epsl, W_e=I.
All words use the same Omega. Reversing an edge reads the same slot and
uses the adjoint. Common edges and prefixes across words share labels.

Let x=sum_g a_g w_g be a finite sum with arbitrary complex cylinder
functions a_g. These are precisely the finite group-algebra sums for
Gamma=C_(n^2)^(G) semidirect G. For its regular trace,
 ||x||2^2=sum_g ||a_g||_(L2(Omega))^2.
Define T0(x)=sum_g E[a_g W_g], a finite sum of bounded operators. Then
 for every such x and every eta in H,
 ||T0(x)eta||^2<=C_q^2||x||2^2||eta||^2,                     (HS1)
 and therefore ||T0(x)||op<=C_q||x||2.                       (HS2)
The same constant works for all eta, including choices depending on x.
T0 has one bounded linear extension L2(L(Gamma))->B(H). This assertion
places no order or completely positive map structure on the Hilbert space L2.

Proof.

3.3.1 The joint law on one reduced path

A reduced path in the free Cayley tree is simple: equality of two prefix
vertices would make a nonempty reduced subword equal to the identity.
An edge-head h_i is one endpoint of the i-th edge. Two heads can coincide
only at a common vertex of consecutive edges. Such a coincidence occurs
exactly for a positive then negative step. Their generators must differ,
otherwise they cancel. Thus the repeated site contributes its row once
and column once. Positive-positive and negative-negative consecutive
steps have distinct heads; negative-positive cannot repeat a head without
a forbidden cancellation. Nonadjacent heads cannot coincide.

If the l slots occupy d sites, prescribing all l labels leaves exactly
n^(2d-l) atoms among n^(2d) at those sites. Every site is uniform and
different sites are independent by definition of Omega. The l labels of
this one path consequently have the full product-uniform law. This is
joint independence along a path, not only pairwise independence. It does
not assert independence of labels for two different paths. The reversed
inverse word is also reduced, so the same argument covers it, in its own
actual slot order; no independent resampling of its labels is made.

3.3.2 Finite cyclic Fourier identity

Fix eta and g. Let Z_g be the finite set of its used sites, d=|Z_g|,
N_g=n^(2d), and v_a=W_g(a)eta for a in the finite atom set Omega_(Z_g).
For each of its N_g original cyclic characters chi define
 z_(g,chi)=T0(chi w_g)eta=N_g^-1 sum_a chi(a)v_a.
A character with a nonzero coordinate outside Z_g has zero image, because
that coordinate is independent and its character has mean zero. For the
characters on Z_g, exact finite Fourier orthogonality gives
 sum_chi chi(a)overline(chi(b))=N_g delta_(a,b).
Expanding each rank-one operator, with all sums finite, proves
 S_(eta,g):=sum_chi |z_(g,chi)><z_(g,chi)|
   =N_g^-1 sum_a |v_a><v_a|
   =E[W_g R_eta W_g*]
   =P_eps1 ... P_epsl(R_eta), R_eta=|eta><eta|.               (HS3)
The last equality follows by integrating the innermost label first, using
Section 3.3.1's full joint law. It is ordered composition; no commutation or
symmetry of P_+ and P_- is assumed. In particular reverse signs use
P_-, not P_+, and reversing the word reverses the appropriate order.
For the empty word, only the trivial lamp character survives and S_(eta,e)=R_eta.

3.3.3 Summability and the positive limit

Since ||R_eta||S2=||eta||^2 and ||P_+||S2=||P_-||S2=q, equation(HS3) yields
 ||S_(eta,g)||S2<=q^|g| ||eta||^2.                            (HS4)
Here q is an operator norm of a channel on S2, not its squared norm.
The frame already contains squared vector coefficients. Consequently
its norm is bounded by q^l, not by a gratuitous replacement q^(2l).
There are 4 choices for the first letter and 3 for each later reduced
letter, so the number of length-l words is4*3^(l-1) for l>=1. Hence
 sum_g ||S_(eta,g)||S2
 <=[1+sum_(l>=1)4*3^(l-1)q^l]||eta||^2
 =[1+4q/(1-3q)]||eta||^2=C_q^2||eta||^2.                    (HS5)
This is absolute convergence of the WORD-GROUPED series in the Banach
space S2(H). Its sum S_eta is positive, since finite positive sums converge
in HS and hence operator norm. Every finite word sum is <=S_eta, and
 ||S_eta||op<=||S_eta||S2<=C_q^2||eta||^2.                    (HS6)
The length-tail bound is explicit:
 ||sum_(|g|>L) S_(eta,g)||op
 <=4q(3q)^L/(1-3q) ||eta||^2, L a nonnegative integer.                        (HS7)
There is no exchange of an infinite Bochner integral with a word sum:
all expectations in(HS3) were finite before(HS5) was used.

Absolute summability in(HS5) does not imply absolute summability of the
ungrouped rank-one terms. For eta nonzero, unitarity and(HS3) give at each word
 sum_chi || |z_(g,chi)><z_(g,chi)| ||S2
   =sum_chi ||z_(g,chi)||^2
   =Tr(S_(eta,g))=E||W_g eta||^2=||eta||^2.                 (HS8)
Its sum over g diverges, even when q<1/3. Likewise E||W_g eta||^2 is not
bounded by q^|g| ||eta||^2 for a nonempty word. Pointwise, W_g is unitary:
 ||W_g(omega)eta||=||eta||,
 ||W_g(omega)R_eta W_g(omega)*||S2=||eta||^2.               (HS9)
Only the norm of the AVERAGED positive frame in(HS4) decays. These identities
are exact counterexamples to the two false strengthenings, not to(HS1).
Infinite trace of S_eta is compatible with its finite HS norm.

For clarity, the ungrouped positive rank-one series DOES converge as a
net of finite subsets in operator norm, without absolute HS convergence.
After a finite subset includes all active characters of all |g|<=L,
its missing positive terms are dominated by the full tail in(HS7). Any
extra retained characters only improve this bound. This uses the finiteness
of each active Fourier block, positivity and(HS7), not an arbitrary exchange
of an absolutely convergent double series. Scalar nonnegative sums can
also be regrouped by Tonelli; they will be bounded explicitly below.

3.3.4 All complex coefficients and mixed Gram terms

For a finite subset F of pairs (g,chi), put z_alpha=z_(g,chi). Every
partial Fourier block is a positive sub-sum of(HS3). Therefore
 S_(eta,F)=sum_(alpha in F)|z_alpha><z_alpha|<=S_eta.          (HS10)
Let A_(eta,F):ell2(F)->H be the complex-linear column operator with these
columns. Then A_(eta,F)A_(eta,F)*=S_(eta,F), whereas A* A is the full
mixed Gram matrix. No cross term for shared prefixes, inverse words,
common lamp coordinates, or equal target outputs has been set to zero.
For every complex coefficient vector c,
 ||sum_(alpha in F)c_alpha z_alpha||^2
 <=||A_(eta,F)||^2 sum_F|c_alpha|^2
 <=C_q^2||eta||^2 sum_F|c_alpha|^2.                         (HS11)
All finite characters outside a used word's sites have image zero; their
coefficients may be retained on the right. After combining repeated
source group terms, Parseval in the actual cyclic-lamp group basis gives
sum|c_alpha|^2=||x||2^2. Equation(HS11) is(HS1). Since eta was arbitrary
with the same bound, taking its unit-sphere supremum proves(HS2).

The scalar Cauchy--Schwarz route has exactly the needed summability:
 for every zeta in H,
 sum_alpha |<z_alpha,zeta>|^2=<S_eta zeta,zeta>
          <=C_q^2||eta||^2||zeta||^2.                      (HS12)
For finite F the sum equals <S_(eta,F)zeta,zeta>, which is bounded
by(HS10). The full equality in(HS12) uses the operator-norm convergence
of that positive finite-subset net to S_eta proved after(HS9). For any c in ell2 of the source
basis, (HS12) implies
 sum_alpha |c_alpha <z_alpha,zeta>|
 <=||c||2 (sum_alpha |<z_alpha,zeta>|^2)^1/2<infinity.        (HS13)
This scalar absolute convergence does not assert absolute vector or
operator convergence of the ungrouped source-basis series. Finite-tail
application of(HS11) gives unconditional vector
norm convergence, and the uniform estimate(HS2) gives operator-norm
convergence of T0 applied to L2 partial sums. Thus its completion is one
well-defined bounded linear map, independent of the enumeration or net
of finite subsets. No common dense countable subset of H is required.

A useful equivalent two-stage Cauchy--Schwarz check makes the exponent
explicit. For one word and arbitrary unit eta,zeta, equations(HS3)-(HS4) give
 E|<W_g eta,zeta>|^2=<S_(eta,g)zeta,zeta><=q^|g|.
Probability-space Cauchy--Schwarz, applied to a_g and this scalar
coefficient, therefore gives
 ||E[a_g W_g]||op<=q^(|g|/2)||a_g||2.                       (HS13a)
For any L2 family (a_g), sequence-space Cauchy--Schwarz gives
 sum_g ||E[a_g W_g]||op
 <=sum_g q^(|g|/2)||a_g||2
 <=(sum_g q^|g|)^(1/2)(sum_g||a_g||2^2)^(1/2)
 =C_q (sum_g||a_g||2^2)^(1/2).                             (HS13b)
For general L2 a_g, E[a_g W_g] is the finite operator sum
sum_(a in Omega_(Z_g)) W_g(a) integral_(omega|Z_g=a) a_g dmu.
These scalar integrals exist since L2 is contained in L1 on this probability
space; (HS13a) follows by L2 density. Thus the WORD-GROUPED
coefficient series is absolutely operator-norm convergent. This is not
absolute convergence of the ungrouped Fourier rank-one series in(HS8).
It requires sum_g q^|g|<infinity, not sum_g q^(|g|/2)<infinity; replacing
the weighted Cauchy--Schwarz step by the latter requirement would wrongly
strengthen the sufficient threshold to q<1/9. Both estimates follow from the same averaged frame identity.

3.3.5 Identification with the spatial paired norm

For the standard-form specialization let N act faithfully normally by L
on H, with conjugation J and r(x)=J L(x*)J. Given v_j in U(N), set
U_j=r(v_j*)=J L(v_j)J. Define V:H tensor bar(H)->S2(H) by
V(eta tensor bar(zeta))=|eta><zeta|, and t(X)=J X* J on S2(H).
Rank-one inner products show that V and t are complex-linear unitaries;
t sends |eta><zeta| to |J zeta><J eta| and t^2=id. Finite-rank density
extends these identities even if H is nonseparable. Directly,
 t(U_j X U_j*)=L(v_j)t(X)L(v_j)*,
so V^-1 t P_+ t^-1 V=n^-1 sum_j L(v_j) tensor overline(L(v_j)).
Both spatial representations are faithful, whence
 q=||P_+||S2=||n^-1 sum_j v_j tensor bar(v_j)||_(N min bar(N)). (HS14)
The map t is not being composed into T0 as a CP map. This is solely a
unitary equivalence of Hilbert-space operators, and uses the conjugate
algebra, not an unannounced opposite-algebra identification.

If phi is ANY normal state on N, its natural-cone vector xi_phi has norm1
and can be substituted for eta in(HS1). Faithfulness is unnecessary: H,L,J
remain the standard form of all N, independent of phi. No state-support
compression or right multiplication on the potentially defective GNS
quotient of phi is used. In fact(HS1) holds for all other target vectors
at once, so the estimate has no hidden state-dependent constant.
This proves Lemma HS. In the construction of Section 3.1, H=H_N and
U_j=r_N(v_j*) give exactly W_g=r_N(B_g*), with the same slots. Equation
(HS14) identifies its q with the one in Proposition M. Hence the actual
compression satisfies ||T0(x)||<=C_q||x||_(2,D), uniformly in every
target vector. Its positivity is supplied by Section 3.2, separately
from this norm estimate. No matrix-degree-independent L2 bound is used.

3.4 The completed reduced kernel and one normal UCP extension

Let p:C_f->C be the regular quotient. For x in ker p choose a_m in A
converging in full norm to x. Then ||a_m||2<=||p(a_m)||->0. Full
boundedness of Phi and the estimate of Lemma HS give Phi(x)=0. Thus
T(p(x))=Phi(x) is one
unital contraction C->L_N(N)'. For positive X in M_k(C), lift sqrt(X)
to Y in M_k(C_f); then T^(k)(X)=Phi^(k)(Y*Y)>=0. This proves UCP at
each k without assuming a CP section of the quotient.

Here is the complete extension argument. Since the trace on D is a
faithful normal state, D is contained in L2(D); A is L2-dense by the
group basis and norm-dense in C. Complete the one L2-bounded map to
 Ttilde:L2(D)->B(H_N), ||Ttilde||<=C_q,
and set theta=Ttilde|D. Norm approximation from A to C also converges
in L2, so theta agrees with T on C and preserves the unit. Its values
are in the norm-closed algebra L_N(N)'. For x in Ball(D), a Kaplansky
net c_alpha in Ball(C) converges strongly to x on source L2(D), hence
||c_alpha-x||2=||(c_alpha-x)hat(1)||->0. Therefore T(c_alpha) converges
to theta(x) in operator norm and ||theta(x)||<=1.

For fixed k and X>=0 in M_k(D), take positive Kaplansky approximants
X_alpha in M_k(C), strongly converging to X. Their entries converge in
L2, and
 ||theta^(k)(X)-T^(k)(X_alpha)||
       <=C_q sum_ij ||X_ij-(X_alpha)_ij||2 ->0.
Thus theta is CP. Once positive entrywise L2 approximants are given,
this last implication does not require an additional uniform norm
bound on them. The bounded unit-ball approximation above served to
prove contractivity. Matrix k remains fixed during the finite sum.

If 0<=x_alpha increases to x in D, normality of its source trace gives
 ||x-x_alpha||2^2<=||x|| tau_D(x-x_alpha)->0.
Consequently theta(x_alpha) increases to theta(x), even in operator
norm, proving normality. These are nets, with no separability assumed.
Uniqueness among normal extensions of T follows from weak density of C.
The moments MOM are unchanged. Normality of the raw pi_f on all H_raw,
and normality of a later Calkin pullback, have not been inferred.

3.5 Finite group models and the faithful C* copy.

First construct the required sequence. For a nontrivial reduced word
in F(s,t), assign distinct vertices0,...,m to its path, read in action
order (rightmost letter first for usual composition of permutations).
For a positive letter u prescribe its permutation to send the current
vertex to the next; for a negative letter prescribe the inverse step.
For each generator these prescriptions are injective partial bijections:
a conflicting domain or range could occur only at an adjacent inverse
pair, which reducedness excludes. Complete each partial bijection to a
permutation of the finite vertex set. The represented word moves0 to m.
Thus every nontrivial word survives a finite quotient. Intersect the
finitely many kernels required to separate all words of length<=2l;
the resulting quotient pi_l:G->G_l is injective on the ball of radius l.

For a finite-support lamp a define
 (pi_l!a)(hbar)=sum_(pi_l(h)=hbar) a(h) modulo n^2.
This respects addition and pi_l!(g.a)=pi_l(g).(pi_l!a), so
 (a,g)->(pi_l!a,pi_l(g))
is an exact homomorphism onto
 Gamma_l=(Z/(n^2))^(G_l) semidirect G_l.
If g!=1 it survives eventually. If g=1 and a!=0, injectivity on a ball
containing its finite support prevents cancellation of distinct lamp
sites, so that element also survives eventually. The regular finite
representations rho_l of Gamma_l have dimension d_l=|Gamma_l| and
tr_l(rho_l(gamma)) eventually equal delta_(gamma,1) for each fixed gamma.
They therefore give the required trace-convergent sequence. Relations,
inverses and repeated words are preserved exactly at every l; the target
cocycle need not factor through these finite quotients.

Use any models as in M. I is a norm-closed two-sided *-ideal in P, by
the tracial inequalities ||ab||2,||ba||2<=||a|| ||b||2 and uniform
operator bounds. The trace tau_P(b)=lim_omega tr_l(b_l) is a tracial
state of P. Cauchy--Schwarz gives tau_P(i)=0 for i in I, so it descends
to a trace tau_U on U=P/I. It is faithful: tau_U(x*x)=0 says exactly
that any representative of x belongs to I.
Let beta:C_f->P be beta(x)=(rho_l(x))_l, and q_tau:P->U the quotient.
Explicitly S_u=beta(w_u), P_(u,j)=beta(p_(1,u,j)), and
b_(u,j)=S_u P_(u,j). Since the p_(1,u,j) are exact finite Fourier
polynomials forming orthogonal partitions, these are the exact model
unitaries and projections used in Proposition M.
The trace limits give tau_U q_tau beta=tau_D p on C_f by norm density.
The regular trace on C is faithful: a positive element with trace0 has
square root killing delta_1, hence every basis vector by the commuting
right regular action. Apply the trace identity to x*x to obtain
                     ker(q_tau beta)=ker p.
There is therefore a faithful unital *-homomorphism
 sigma_C:C->U, sigma_C p=q_tau beta,
 sigma_C(d_(u,j))=q_tau(b_(u,j)).                        (COPY)
This establishes the C* norm, not just an L2 identity. It does not infer
operator norm convergence of finite blocks from trace convergence.

3.6 A trace-GNS return and a map on the whole Calkin algebra.

In the trace GNS
spaces define the densely specified map W:L2(D)->L2(U,tau_U) on the dense C by
                       W(chat)=sigma_C(c)^hat.
Trace preservation makes W an isometry on the completion, with norm1
on all L2(D). Right multiplication R_U(a) on L2(U,tau_U) is bounded by
||a|| and has adjoint R_U(a*); the tracial property gives these assertions
even though U is only a C* algebra. For c in C,
 W R_D(c)=R_U(sigma_C(c))W, W R_D(c*)=R_U(sigma_C(c*))W.
Thus W intertwines both right multiplication and its adjoint. For x in U,
the compression
W*L_U(x)W commutes with R_D(C), hence R_D(D), since L_U(x) commutes with
right multiplication and range(W) reduces R_U(sigma_C(C)). The standard
commutant theorem gives a UNIQUE element F_U(x) in D with
                   L_D(F_U(x))=W*L_U(x)W.
Compression at every matrix level proves UCP. The same dense-vector
calculation gives L_U(sigma_C(c))W=W L_D(c), so F_U(sigma_C(c))=c for c in C.
No assertion that U is a von Neumann algebra, or that a normal embedding
D->U has been constructed, is used.

Let q_c:B(H_c)->Q_c be the Calkin quotient and let
Delta:B(H_c)->P be diagonal block compression. It is UCP and sends
compacts into the norm-null block ideal c0 subset I: a rank-one block norm
is ||eta_l||||zeta_l||->0, and finite-rank norm approximation finishes.
Consequently F_U q_tau Delta kills K(H_c) and defines a UCP map on all
Q_c, by positive matrix quotient lifts:
 F_Q(q_c(X))=F_U(q_tau(Delta(X))),
 F_Q(j(b))=F_U(q_tau(b)) for every b in P.              (ALL-Q)
Put T_v=theta F_Q. COPY, MOM and ALL-Q give simultaneously every
specified moment and T_v(j(I))=0, proving M. The same choices of models,
omega,W,F_U,F_Q and theta serve all moments and every later cut.

Only range containment is needed for theta and T_v. Source surjectivity
of a return, when separately proved, would not imply target surjectivity.
Nor is a prescribed reverse assignment d_(u,j)->j(b_(u,j)) assumed
positive. The faithful copy is in P/I; no UCP lift of it to P or Q_c
is an input. Normality of theta does not make the pullback of T_v to
B(H_c) normal. These distinctions prevent strengthening M beyond its proof.

4. TRANSFER D TO THE ORIGINAL C* ALGEBRA

Proposition D (proposed detector). Assumptions and conclusion.

Let B be a nonzero exact C* algebra, possibly nonunital and nonseparable,
V=B**, and kappa:B->V the canonical inclusion. Assume G has supplied
a nonzero e in Proj(V), N=eVe in normal standard form L_N,r_N on H_N,
relative unitaries v_1,...,v_n, and a normal state phi with unit cone
vector xi such that

4.1 One fixed state and its normal left variable

Set alpha:V->B(H_N), alpha(X)=L_N(eXe). This is normal UCP, with
alpha(1_V)=1_(H_N), and its range commutes with T(Q_c). It is generally
not a *-representation. We construct the needed commuting dilation
while retaining the compression as a UCP map.
On V odot Q_c odot H_N use the sesquilinear form, linear in its first
variable,
 <X tensor z tensor eta,Y tensor w tensor zeta>
     =<alpha(Y*X)T(w*z)eta,zeta>.
For finite families A=[alpha(X_i*X_j)] and D=[T(z_i*z_j)] are positive
matrices and each entry of A commutes with each entry of D. Choose
S=A^(1/2) in the matrix C* algebra generated by alpha(V). Functional
calculus stays in that matrix algebra, so every entry of S commutes
with every entry of D. This choice is essential; an arbitrary S with
S*S=A need not have that property. With D_l=diag_i(S_li),
 [A_ij D_ij]=sum_l D_l* D D_l>=0.
This proves positivity, retaining all cross terms; it does not assume
entries within A commute among themselves. Applying the same argument
to [alpha(X_i*(||X||^2-X*X)X_j)] proves that left multiplication by X
on the first factor is bounded by ||X||. The corresponding positive
matrix for T bounds multiplication on the second factor by ||z||.
Quotienting the null space and completing gives commuting unital
*-representations rho:V->B(K), nu:Q_c->B(K), and the isometry
J eta=[1 tensor1 tensor eta], with
 J*rho(X)nu(z)J=alpha(X)T(z).                              (COMP)
Thus J xi defines a state Omega_V on V tensor_max Q_c. No assertion about
minimal continuity of this product representation is used.

The left representation is normal: on finite sums of the dense vectors
rho(X_i)nu(z_i)J eta_i, all rho(A)-coefficients are finite sums of
<alpha(X_j* A X_i)T(z_j* z_i)eta_i,eta_j>, which are normal in A.
Approximation of arbitrary vectors in norm, using ||rho(A)||<=||A||,
gives normal coefficients because V_* is norm closed in V*. This uses
normal alpha and fixed T-values, never normality in the z variable.

Pull Omega_V back through kappa tensor_max id to original B, obtaining
 Psi(b tensor z)=<L_N(e kappa(b)e)T(z)xi,xi>.             (STATE)
The pullback map need not be injective. The restriction is a state,
not merely a substate: for a positive contractive approximate identity
b_a in B, normality gives Psi(b_a tensor1)->phi(e)=1. Hence its norm is1.
There is one fixed vector state and commuting dilation throughout.
Only the left V-variable is called normal; no predual/normality is
being assigned to the C* algebra Q_c or to the whole maximal C* product.

4.2 Finite normal tests and original-B coefficients

For k=1,...,n define the normal slice in the same state
 f_k(X)=n^-1<L_N(eXe)r_N(v_k*)xi,xi>,  ||f_k||<=1/n.
Equivalently it is the Omega_V-slice at j(b_(u,k)) for either u. Its
normality follows from L_N and compression, since r_N(v_k*)xi is a
fixed vector. In canonical V=B**, these normal functionals belong to
the specified predual B*, so Goldstine applies to them.

After fixing Psi, T, all v_j and all these tests, Goldstine says that
kappa(Ball(B)) is sigma(B**,B*)-dense in Ball(B**). Each v_j has norm1
in V even though its unit is e. Therefore choose a_j in Ball(B) with
 |f_k(kappa(a_j)-v_j)|<gamma=1/(256n) for every k and j.   (GOLD)
Each j is an elementwise choice against one finite common list. For
example, the inequalities define a weak*-open neighborhood of v_j;
Goldstine puts a point of kappa(Ball(B)) in it. There are only finitely
many j, so all choices can be made before forming the tensor below.
No sequence or metrizability is assumed. The chosen elements belong
to B itself, not only its unitization. They need not be unitaries,
supported on e, multiplicative images of v_j, or a completely contractive
linear interpolation on their span. No products of the a_j are being
approximated, and no matrix-level or UCP-map approximation is claimed.
Kaplansky density and local reflexivity are not used in this selection.

Using the same coefficients set
 y=(1/4)sum_(u=s,t;j)(a_j tensor d_(u,j)+a_j* tensor d_(u,j)*),
 t0=(1/4)sum_(u,j)(a_j tensor b_(u,j)+a_j* tensor b_(u,j)*),
 u0=(id_B tensor j)(t0),
in B odot C, B odot P and B odot Q_c, respectively.
The expression with v_j instead of kappa(a_j) in Omega_V has value r.
For each of the 2n nonadjoint terms, M-IN and GOLD bound its state-value
error by gamma. Adjoint paired terms have conjugate values because
Omega_V and Psi are positive states. Thus no separate assertion that
the f_k-values of a_j* approximate v_j* is required. We get
 |Psi(u0)-r|<(1/4)(2)(2n)gamma=1/256,
 Psi(u0)>251/256.                                       (LOWER)
The approximation has preserved specified values of one fixed state,
not the entire original joint law or a small paired tensor norm for a_j.

4.3 An independent spatial upper bound

Take a faithful nondegenerate representation pi_B of original B and
the faithful regular crossed-product representation of C on the fibers
ell2(G,L2(Omega) tensor H_B). On its h fiber define
 C_u(h)=sum_j L(sigma_(h^-1)(p_(1,u,j))) tensor pi_B(a_j).
The lamp projections are orthogonal and commute with the coefficient
operators, so every C_u(h) is a contraction. With lambda_u the left
shift on G, Z_u=lambda_u C_u represents sum_j a_j tensor d_(u,j).
At a fiber h, Z_u xi has norm at most ||xi(u^-1h)||, and Z_u* xi has
norm at most ||xi(uh)||. Hence the norm of sum_u(Z_u+Z_u*) is bounded
by the scalar adjacency norm of the four-regular tree.
For completeness, its weight w(h)=3^(-|h|/2) gives row weighted-sum
ratio 2sqrt(3) off the root and 4/sqrt(3) at the root. Weighted
Cauchy--Schwarz gives |Af(h)|^2<=2sqrt(3) sum_(k~h)
w(h)/w(k)|f(k)|^2; summing h and applying the same ratio bound gives
||A||<=2sqrt(3). The positive weight need not itself lie in ell2.
Thus in these faithful spatial representations
 ||y||_(B min C)<=sqrt(3)/2.                            (SOURCE-MIN)
This holds for every tuple a_j in Ball(B), irrespective of its quality
of approximation to v_j. It is not weak*-continuity of a tensor norm.

Faithfulness of sigma and injectivity of the min tensor norm give
 s=||(id_B tensor q_tau)(t0)||_(B min U)
  =||(id_B tensor sigma)(y)||=||y||min<=sqrt(3)/2.        (QUOT)
No small min norm for uncut u0 in the Calkin algebra is asserted.
In contrast, any commuting representations pi_B,nu_P of B and P give
||sum_j pi_B(a_j)nu_P(P_(u,j))||<=1 by orthogonal blocks. Multiplying
by nu_P(S_u) preserves the bound, so averaging two contractions and
their adjoints gives ||t0||_(B max P)<=1 and ||u0||max<=1. (MAX)
This uses no injectivity of an arbitrary maximal tensor inclusion.

4.4 A finite ideal cut preserving that same state

Set eta=1/256. This is the only use of exactness: for the norm-closed
two-sided ideal I of P,

4.6 Scope and order of the approximations

Goldstine selects finitely many elements a_j of Ball(B), testing only
normal functionals in the canonical V variable. It approximates neither
matrices nor a UCP map nor their multiplication. SOURCE-MIN supplies

selects a finite element of B odot I for one quotient-norm approximation.
The fixed state is preserved by an exact all-I identity, not by a
weak* limit of its right-hand variable. No faithful normal state,
support cut, local-reflexivity hypothesis, maximal-inclusion isometry,
or equality of the min and state-evaluating representations is added.
D is a conditional transfer result under exactly the displayed G/M
package and exactness of B. Propositions G and M supply that package in
Sections 2-3, and Section 1
derives exactness from the original hypothesis. This proves Proposition D.

APPENDIX A. THE SCOPE OF BLOCK-IDEAL ANNIHILATION

For this infinite source, trace convergence forces d_l->infinity:
for any m distinct group elements, their model matrices have normalized
Hilbert-Schmidt Gram tending to I_m, hence rank m<=d_l^2 eventually.
Adding one trivial representation to each model therefore preserves the
trace limit, since its weight is1/(d_l+1). The same trace-kernel proof
then supplies a faithful sigma_C for the augmented models. The added
block projection p belongs to the new trace ideal I but is not compact:
it fixes infinitely many orthonormal added vectors and has essential
norm1. Applying Section 3.6 for these very models supplies the same simultaneous
moment/all-I statement used by Section 4.4. No extra dimension assumption is needed.

In any Stinespring T_v(a)=W*nu(a)W, the guaranteed annihilation subspace is
 K_block=closure span nu(j(P))W H_N.
For i in I and b in P, ib in I and T_v(j((ib)*(ib)))=0 give
nu(j(i))nu(j(b))W=0. With a commuting left action rho this also holds
on closure span rho(V)nu(j(P))WH_N. This is not the whole Q_c-generated
space. Indeed choose an isometry S of H_c onto the infinite added
subspace, z=q_c(S), pbar=j(p). Then
 z*z=1, zz*=pbar, z*pbar z=1,
 T_v(pbar)=0, but T_v(z*pbar z)=1.
Thus full-dilation annihilation is false and unused. Section 4.4 needs
only cbc-b in I for b in P. It uses its actual exactness-selected cut;
replacing that cut by the diagnostic p alone would not establish
||u||min<7/8.
This control makes the logical boundary explicit, without assuming
multiplicativity, normality or surjectivity of the whole-Q return.
